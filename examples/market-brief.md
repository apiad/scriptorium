## Why we wrote this

::: {.lead}
Conviction is cheap; this document is the exercise of pricing it against
evidence. Every assumption behind the go-to-market plan is written down here and
checked against a public number.
:::

::: {.two-col}
The thesis is set: the wedge is a frictionless self-serve tier that converts
into mid-market expansion. That decision rested on a reading of the landscape
that lived in scattered notes. The purpose of this brief is to submit each of
those notes to verification, and to be honest about the ranges we cannot pin
down precisely.

We chose four lenses — market size, competitive structure, regional pull, and
regulatory exposure — because each forces a different posture. Where we estimate,
we say so; where two public figures disagree, we show both and take the
conservative one. Each section closes with what the slice means for the plan.
:::

## Three postures, one seam

::: {.lead}
Incumbents own the enterprise; challengers crowd the free tier. The defensible
ground is the mid-market seam between them.
:::

::: card-grid three
::: card accent
### Incumbents
Own enterprise procurement and the data team. Slow, expensive, over-featured for
a ten-person team. We do not compete here.
:::
::: card amber
### Challengers
Free tiers and viral loops. Great at acquisition, weak at expansion. They train
the market we convert.
:::
::: card emerald
### The seam
Mid-market teams priced out of one and outgrown from the other. This is the
wedge, and it is under-served on purpose.
:::
:::

::: pull-quote {src="— VP Strategy, internal review"}
The window between "too small for enterprise" and "too complex for free" is open
for maybe eighteen months. After that it closes from both sides.
:::

| Segment | Incumbent grip | Our fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | **Strong** | Low | Procurement moats, not product |
| Mid-market | Weak | **Strong** | The wedge; fastest conversion |
| SMB / free | Contested | Medium | Feeds the funnel, low ARPU |

## Land, expand, defend

::: timeline
::: tier accent {pill="Phase 1" title="Land" when="Q1–Q2 2026"}
Ship the self-serve tier; win 20 design partners in the mid-market seam. Success
is activation rate, not revenue.
:::
::: tier amber {pill="Phase 2" title="Expand" when="Q3 2026"}
Turn on usage-based expansion; instrument the net-retention loop. Diversify off
the top-three accounts.
:::
::: tier emerald {pill="Phase 3" title="Defend" when="Q4 2026"}
Deepen the moat with integrations the challengers cannot match. Enter Western
Europe once the motion is proven.
:::
:::

::: cta emerald {title="What this means"}
Defend the self-serve motion, plant in North America first, and make account
diversification a first-year objective. The window is open — but not for long.
